# NutriBrick presentation script

Lines in square brackets are stage directions, not words to say.
Word count and timing are at the end.

---

[Hero slide]

Our product is called NutriBrick. It is a snack for primary school aged children,
shaped like a building brick.

The problem we set out to solve is that a lot of Australian children are not
eating well. Our research found that over a million children in Australia are
unhealthy, and that 95% are not meeting their daily macronutrient targets.

The part that matters is why. It is not that parents do not know what is healthy,
because most of them do. The problem is getting children to actually eat it. The
healthy snack gets left, something sweet gets swapped in instead, and the same
thing happens the next day.

[Dinner should not be a negotiation]

Younger children mostly decide what they will eat based on how it looks and how
it feels in their hands, rather than on what is in it. So a snack that is good
for them but looks boring tends to lose, no matter how much a parent explains it.

[Build it. Stack it. Eat it.]

NutriBrick is designed around that. It is a food bite about the size of a Duplo
brick, with studs on top that interlock properly, so the bricks stack together.
Children build something out of them, and then eat it.

[Don't play with your food]

One thing we had to think about is that a lot of parents do not want their kids
playing with food. That rule exists for a reason. Playing with food normally
means it gets mashed, hidden or dropped, and none of it actually gets eaten.

With our product, the building is what leads to it being eaten. The food is not
wasted, so the reason behind the rule does not really apply here.

[The snack aisle, honestly compared]

We compared it against what is already available. Frozen kids meals need a
freezer, a microwave and about twelve minutes. Meal pouches are reasonable
nutritionally, but there is nothing for a child to do with them. There are
already lolly blocks shaped like bricks, but the shape is the whole product and
the inside is sugar. We could not find an existing product that was both
something to play with and genuinely nutritious.

[One brick. 45g. Here is all of it.]

One brick is 45 grams. It contains 203 calories, 13 grams of protein, 4 grams of
fibre, and a full day of vitamin C, A and D for a young child.

There are seven ingredients. Rolled oats, sunflower seed butter, milk protein,
dates, chia and flaxseed, and cocoa and cinnamon for flavour. The only added part
is the three vitamins, because a bite this size cannot fit a full day of them
from food alone.

It is nut free, because the bind comes from sunflower seed butter rather than
peanut butter, which means it can go to school. There is no gelatine, no refined
sugar and no artificial colours.

These figures are our targets for the recipe. It has not been lab tested, because
it is still a concept.

[Why nutrient density matters]

Nutrient density matters here because young children have small stomachs and high
nutrient needs, so each bite has to do more work than an adult's would. It is
meant as a snack, not a meal replacement.

[How it is made]

It is made in four steps with no baking. The dry ingredients and the seed butter
are mixed into a firm dough, pressed into a brick shaped mould, chilled until it
holds its shape, then packed. It keeps about five days in the fridge, or two
months in the freezer.

[Pricing]

We are planning on $2 per brick, sold in packs of seven. That is $14 for a week
at one a day. There is no subscription, and it does not need freezer space.

Each pack also includes plans for a new build, with a different design each time.
That is the part we think would bring people back for another pack, rather than
buying it once.

[Closing slide]

NutriBrick is a concept at this stage. We are a Year 10 team working through the
Future Anything program, we have not produced a sellable product, and this
website is our pitch for the idea.

What we are confident about is the reasoning behind it. Children decide with
their eyes and their hands first, and most healthy snacks ignore that. Ours is
built around it.

## Notes for presenting

- Slow down through the "don't play with your food" section. State the rule
  first, then explain why it does not apply. If it is rushed the point is lost.
- Let the 3D brick finish loading before talking about it.
- Say "building brick" rather than "LEGO". LEGO and Duplo are trademarks, so it
  is safer not to lean on them in a pitch.
- Check the 95% statistic before presenting and have the source ready. It is the
  number a judge is most likely to ask about.
- If running over time, cut the "how it is made" section. It is the least
  important part.
- If asked whether it is a real product, say straight away that it is a concept,
  the nutrition figures are recipe targets, and the website is the pitch.
- If asked about the build plans, they are a card in each pack with a design to
  make from the seven bricks, different in every pack.
