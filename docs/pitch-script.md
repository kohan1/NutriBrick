# NutriBrick — presentation script

343 spoken words: 2:38 at a measured 130 wpm, 2:22 at 145, 2:09 at a brisk 160 —
so 2½–3 minutes once you add the pauses. Cues in **[brackets]**
tell you what to have on screen. Not served by the site — the Pages workflow
only publishes `index.html`.

---

**[Hero — the 3D brick turning]**

Most Australian kids aren't eating enough of what they need. Our research found
over a million children in Australia are unhealthy, and that ninety-five percent
aren't meeting their daily macronutrient targets.

But if you've ever tried to get a six-year-old to finish their vegetables, you
know the real problem isn't information. Parents already know what's healthy.

**[Scroll: "Dinner should not be a negotiation"]**

Dinner is a negotiation. The snack goes in the bin untouched. Something sweet
gets traded in. Nobody wins, and tomorrow you do it again.

Nagging is not a nutrition plan.

**[Scroll: "Build it. Stack it. Eat it." — let the tower render]**

So we made this. NutriBrick — a food bite the size of a building brick, with
studs that actually interlock. Kids build a tower. Then they eat the tower.

**[Scroll: "The snack aisle, honestly compared"]**

Here's why it's different from what's already on the shelf. Frozen kid meals
need a freezer and twelve minutes you don't have. Meal pouches hide the
nutrition where nobody can see it. And novelty block lollies get the fun right
and the inside wrong — it's sugar.

NutriBrick is the only one that puts play and nutrition in the same bite.

**[Scroll: "One brick. 45g. Here is all of it."]**

And we're not hiding anything. One brick is forty-five grams — two hundred and
three calories, thirteen grams of protein, four grams of fibre, and a full day
of vitamins C, A and D.

Seven ingredients: oats, sunflower seed butter, milk protein, dates, chia and
flaxseed, cocoa and cinnamon. Nut-free, so it clears the school gate. No
gelatine, no refined sugar, no artificial colours.

**[Scroll: pricing — the seven day-bricks]**

Our target price is two dollars a brick, sold as a pack of seven. One for each
day of the week — fourteen dollars. No subscription, no freezer space, no
leftovers to scrape.

**[Scroll: "A Year 10 idea, built properly"]**

We're a Year 10 team and NutriBrick is still a concept — this site is the pitch
for it.

But the insight is simple, and we think it's right. Kids already decide what
they'll eat by how it looks and how it feels in their hands. Every other healthy
snack asks them to ignore that.

We designed for it instead.

Make the healthy thing the fun thing, and you don't have to negotiate.

Thank you.

## Delivery notes

- **Land the three beats.** "Nagging is not a nutrition plan," "they eat the
  tower," and "make the healthy thing the fun thing." Pause after each.
- **Let the 3D brick do the work.** After "So we made this," stop talking for a
  beat and let them look at it.
- **Say "building brick", not "LEGO".** LEGO and Duplo are trademarks. The site
  says "building brick" and "Duplo-brick-sized" as a size reference only; in a
  spoken pitch "building brick" is safer and sounds no weaker.
- **Check the 95% figure before you present.** It's the one claim a judge is
  most likely to question, and it's the one I can't verify for you — have the
  source ready, or soften it to "most children aren't meeting their targets".
- **If you're short on time,** cut the snack-aisle comparison. It's the section
  the slides carry best on their own.
- **Likely question — "is it actually a product?"** Answer plainly: it's a
  concept, the nutrition figures are formulation targets, and the site is the
  pitch. Saying that first is stronger than being caught on it.
